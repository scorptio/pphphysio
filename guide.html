Practical Guidance

Seeking Care, Daily Habits & Common Questions

Know when to book an assessment, when to seek urgent medical care, and how everyday habits support your long-term pelvic health.

Physiotherapy Consultation

When to Book a Pelvic Health Physiotherapy Assessment/Consultation

Reach out to a pelvic-health physiotherapist or your healthcare provider if you notice:

  • Leaking urine or stool at six weeks postpartum or beyond
  • A bulging or heavy sensation in the vagina
  • Pain during sex that continues past the early healing weeks
  • A visible or persistent abdominal muscle gap
  • Ongoing pelvic, back, or hip pain
  • Difficulty controlling gas or stool
  • Any pelvic symptom that affects your confidence, comfort, or daily activities

You can reach out as soon as symptoms appear, even if they feel mild, rather than waiting for them to become severe. Many physiotherapists recommend a pelvic-floor check even when you feel generally well, since an early assessment can catch small issues and guide safe exercise as you return to activity. In some places, a pelvic-floor review is a standard part of postpartum care. Elsewhere, you may need to ask your doctor, midwife, or obstetrician for a referral.

Urgent Medical Care

When to Seek Prompt Medical Attention

Although many postpartum pelvic-health concerns can be addressed through physiotherapy, some symptoms may indicate a medical problem that requires prompt assessment by a healthcare professional.

Seek prompt medical attention if you experience:

  • Heavy vaginal bleeding, such as soaking a pad within an hour, or passing large blood clots.
  • A fever of 38°C or higher, chills, or foul-smelling vaginal discharge.
  • Severe or worsening abdominal, pelvic, or perineal pain.
  • Difficulty urinating or being unable to empty your bladder.
  • New or worsening redness, swelling, or discharge around a C-section incision or perineal wound.

Seek emergency medical attention for chest pain, difficulty breathing, fainting, seizures, or a severe headache accompanied by vision changes.

These symptoms should not be managed by booking a routine pelvic-health physiotherapy appointment alone. Contact your healthcare provider or seek urgent medical care. If you are experiencing an emergency, call 911.

Remember: Pelvic-health physiotherapy can support postpartum recovery, but it does not replace medical assessment when warning signs are present.

Finding Support

What to Consider When Choosing a Provider

A few things to look for when seeking pelvic-health physiotherapy:

  • Specific training in pelvic health. Look for a physiotherapist with postgraduate training or certification in this area, since internal pelvic assessment calls for training beyond a general physiotherapy degree.
  • Comfort with internal assessment, if offered. Some evaluations include an internal vaginal or rectal exam to assess muscle tone and coordination directly. This is always done with your consent and can be discussed or declined beforehand.
  • A plan tailored to you. Your history, delivery type, symptoms, and goals should shape your treatment plan, rather than a generic program applied to everyone.
  • Clear communication. A good provider explains what they are doing, why, and what results you can realistically expect over time.

Your First Visit

What to Expect From a Pelvic-Health Physiotherapy Session

Your first appointment usually begins with a conversation about your pregnancy, delivery, symptoms, and daily routine. From there, your physiotherapist may:

  • Observe your posture, breathing pattern, and how you move
  • Check your abdominal muscles for separation (diastasis recti)
  • Assess pelvic floor strength and coordination, which may include an internal exam with your consent
  • Build a home exercise plan suited to your body and schedule
  • Offer guidance on returning to exercise, lifting your baby, and daily activities

Sessions are typically spaced out over weeks to months, with follow-up visits to track your progress and adjust your plan. Consistency at home between sessions makes a real difference to your results, so your physiotherapist will likely give you simple exercises to repeat daily.

Daily Wellness

Everyday Habits That Support Recovery

Alongside professional guidance, small daily habits can support your healing:

  • Practice gentle pelvic floor contractions as your physiotherapist advises, building up gradually
  • Mind your posture when feeding, carrying, or lifting your baby
  • Breathe steadily through a lift, letting your exhale carry you through the effort
  • Stay ahead of constipation with fluids, fiber, and movement, since straining adds pressure to the pelvic floor
  • Rest when you can, since healing tissue needs recovery time alongside exercise
  • Return to higher-impact exercise gradually, and check in with your physiotherapist before resuming running, jumping, or heavy lifting
Getting Started

How and Where to Access Pelvic-Health Physiotherapy

You can usually access pelvic-health physiotherapy through:

  • A referral from your obstetrician, midwife, or general practitioner
  • Direct booking with a private pelvic-health physiotherapy clinic in your area
  • Hospital-based maternity or postpartum physiotherapy programs, where available
  • Telehealth consultations, which many clinics now offer for initial guidance and follow-up

A Final Note

Postpartum pelvic floor symptoms are common, treatable, and nothing to feel ashamed of. Your body has done something remarkable, and it deserves the same care and attention during recovery as it received during pregnancy. Reaching out early, even for mild symptoms, can make a difference in your comfort, confidence, and daily life.

You deserve support throughout your recovery. Pelvic-health concerns deserve attention.

Evidence Over Misconceptions

Common Myths About Postpartum Pelvic Health

Myth 1

“Leaking urine after childbirth is just part of being a mother.”

Fact: Urinary leakage is common after childbirth, but that does not mean it should be dismissed as something mothers simply have to live with. A systematic review of 24 studies involving more than 35,000 women found that approximately 31% experienced urinary incontinence between six weeks and one year postpartum (Moossdorff-Steinhauser et al., 2021). Although common, urinary leakage can be assessed and may improve with appropriate support, including pelvic-health physiotherapy.

Myth 2

“It will eventually go away on its own, so I don't need help.”

Fact: Some postpartum pelvic-floor symptoms improve naturally as the body recovers, but others can persist and benefit from assessment and treatment. In a study examining postpartum urinary incontinence, more than half of the women who did not seek professional help expected their symptoms to improve over time (Moossdorff-Steinhauser et al., 2021). You do not need to wait until symptoms become severe before seeking support.

Myth 3

“If I had a Caesarean birth, I don't need to worry about my pelvic floor.”

Fact: Caesarean birth may reduce the risk of certain pelvic-floor concerns compared with vaginal birth, but it does not eliminate the possibility of developing symptoms. Pregnancy itself places pressure on the pelvic floor, and changes can occur regardless of delivery method. A systematic review and meta-analysis found higher rates of urinary incontinence, pelvic organ prolapse, and anal incontinence following vaginal delivery compared with Caesarean delivery (Barca et al., 2021). Pelvic-health support can therefore be beneficial after either type of birth.

Myth 4

“Doing Kegel exercises is the solution to every pelvic-floor problem.”

Fact: Kegel exercises involve contracting the pelvic-floor muscles and may help individuals experiencing reduced muscle strength. However, not every pelvic-floor concern is caused by weakness. Some individuals experience increased muscle tension, difficulty relaxing, or problems with muscle coordination. Treatment should be based on an individualized assessment rather than assuming everyone needs strengthening exercises. A pelvic-health physiotherapist can help determine which approaches are appropriate for your needs.
