Birth Pathways & Rehabilitation

Postpartum Recovery After Vaginal Birth Versus Caesarean Birth

The type of birth you experience can influence your postpartum recovery, including how your pelvic floor heals. However, both vaginal and Caesarean births involve physical changes, and both require time for recovery.

Vaginal Delivery

Recovery After Vaginal Birth

During vaginal birth, the pelvic-floor muscles and surrounding tissues stretch to allow the baby to pass through the birth canal. This can contribute to temporary or ongoing pelvic-floor concerns, particularly if there is significant stretching, tearing, or an assisted delivery using forceps or vacuum.

Some individuals may experience:

  • Perineal soreness, swelling, or discomfort.
  • Urinary leakage when coughing, sneezing, or exercising.
  • Pelvic heaviness or pressure.
  • Changes in bowel control.
  • Discomfort during intercourse.

Recovery depends on several factors, including the extent of perineal injury, the type of delivery, and individual healing.

Caesarean Delivery

Recovery After Caesarean Birth

Although Caesarean birth does not involve the baby passing through the vaginal birth canal, it is still major abdominal surgery. The abdominal muscles and surrounding tissues require time to heal following the procedure.

Some individuals may experience:

  • Abdominal incision soreness and tenderness.
  • Reduced abdominal strength during early recovery.
  • Difficulty with movements such as lifting, bending, or getting out of bed.
  • Scar sensitivity or discomfort.
  • Pelvic-floor symptoms that developed during pregnancy.

Caesarean birth may reduce the risk of certain pelvic-floor concerns, particularly stress urinary incontinence and pelvic organ prolapse, compared with vaginal birth. However, it does not eliminate this risk because pregnancy itself places pressure on the pelvic floor through increased abdominal weight, hormonal changes, and changes in posture.

Individualized Support

How Can Pelvic-Health Physiotherapy Help?

Pelvic-health physiotherapy can support recovery after either type of birth. Treatment is individualized based on your birth experience, healing, symptoms, and personal goals.

A physiotherapist may assess pelvic-floor muscle function, abdominal recovery, scar mobility when appropriate, and movement patterns. They can help guide a gradual return to everyday activities and exercise.

Remember: There is no single recovery timeline. Whether you have a vaginal or Caesarean birth, you deserve support if you experience pelvic-floor symptoms or concerns during recovery.

Specialized Care

What Is Pelvic-Health Physiotherapy?

Pelvic-health physiotherapy is a specialized branch of physical therapy focused on the muscles, nerves, and connective tissue of the pelvic floor. A pelvic-health physiotherapist is trained to assess how well these muscles contract, relax, and coordinate, and to build a recovery plan suited to your body and your goals.

Pelvic Floor Muscle Training (PFMT)

Often called Kegel exercises, to rebuild strength, endurance, and coordination.

Manual Therapy

Including gentle internal and external techniques to ease tight tissue and improve mobility.

Biofeedback

Using a sensor to help you see and feel whether you are contracting the right muscles.

Electrical Stimulation

Which can support muscle activation when a voluntary contraction feels difficult to find.

Posture, Breathing & Movement Education

Education on posture, breathing, and safe movement for lifting, exercising, and daily baby care.

This kind of physiotherapy is considered a low-risk, non-invasive first option for many postpartum pelvic concerns, and it’s recommended as a starting point before considering more invasive treatments.

Beyond Kegels

It’s Not Always About Strengthening

Our pelvic floor needs to do more than simply contract. These muscles must also relax, stretch, and coordinate with other muscles during everyday activities.

Some individuals experience reduced muscle strength, while others experience increased muscle tension or difficulty relaxing their pelvic floor. Others may experience difficulties with muscle coordination or a combination of these concerns.

This is why pelvic-health physiotherapy is individualized. A physiotherapist can assess how your pelvic-floor muscles function, including their strength, ability to relax, flexibility, and coordination. Based on your assessment, they can determine which treatment approaches are most appropriate for your symptoms and recovery goals.

Strengthening Exercises

To improve pelvic-floor muscle strength and support bladder and bowel control when weakness is identified.

Relaxation Techniques

To help release excessive muscle tension and improve the ability of the pelvic floor to relax.

Coordination Exercises

To improve how the pelvic-floor muscles work together with breathing, abdominal muscles, and everyday movements.

Manual Therapy

To address muscle tension, discomfort, scar sensitivity, or restricted tissue mobility when appropriate.

Education and Movement Guidance

To support comfortable movement, lifting, posture, and a gradual return to physical activity.

Not everyone needs the same exercises. Performing pelvic-floor contractions incorrectly or when they are not appropriate for your symptoms may not address the underlying concern. Your physiotherapist can adjust your treatment plan as your symptoms, function, and recovery progress.
